# Set up the nonprofit — the exact steps, in order, with what each costs

*Not legal or tax advice. Fees are the current published ones (Utah FY2026 schedule; IRS user fees); verify at filing. A nonprofit has no owners — nobody's name goes on it as an owner, including yours; people sit on its board. That changes the money terms; see the end.*

| # | Step | Where | Cost · time | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Name and purpose, one sentence** | on paper | $0 · tonight | *"To reduce impaired-driving deaths and injuries in Utah by ensuring a safe ride is reliably available at closing time, and to measure the result with the state."* Public safety is a recognised charitable purpose; the sentence is what the IRS reads |
| 2 | **Three board members** | people you name | $0 · this week | Utah requires a board; three is the working minimum. Andrea can sit on it. A majority should be people who are not paid by the organisation and not related to each other — the IRS looks at this on the 1023 |
| 3 | **Articles of Incorporation — Utah nonprofit corporation** | Utah Division of Corporations, online | **$59** · same day online | Must include the 501(c)(3) purpose clause and the dissolution clause (assets go to another charity if it closes) or the IRS bounces the exemption. Use the state's own template |
| 4 | **EIN** | IRS, online | $0 · minutes | Needed for the bank account and the 1023 |
| 5 | **Bylaws and the first board meeting** | your table | $0 · one evening | Adopt bylaws; elect officers; adopt a conflict-of-interest policy (the 1023 asks); authorise the bank account and the 1023 filing. Minutes in the file |
| 6 | **Bank account** | any bank | $0–$25 · one visit | In the nonprofit's name and EIN. Andrea's money never touches a personal account |
| 7 | **Federal exemption — Form 1023-EZ** | IRS, online | **$275** · typically weeks to a few months | Eligible only if projected gross receipts stay ≤ $50k a year for three years and assets ≤ $250k. **A nonprofit that intends to own a $750k building fails the asset test → full Form 1023, $600, several months.** Decide which before filing: the EZ fits a nonprofit that *leases* bays and holds the measurement contract; the full 1023 fits one that owns the depot |
| 8 | **Utah charitable solicitation registration** | Utah Division of Consumer Protection | modest fee · before asking anyone for money | Required before soliciting donations in Utah. Andrea's $50k as a *loan* is not a solicitation; a public campaign is |
| 9 | **State tax exemption** | Utah State Tax Commission | $0 · after the IRS letter | Sales-tax exemption follows the federal determination |
| 10 | **The measurement memorandum** | Utah Highway Safety Office | $0 · the email in folder 04 | The nonprofit is the party to it. This is the document that makes a CDFI or a grant officer say yes |

**Total to exist and apply:** about **$334** (EZ) or **$659** (full 1023), plus a bank visit and one evening. The determination letter is the long pole — weeks at best, months for the full form.

## Andrea's $50k, in a nonprofit — what changes

| The for-profit terms (earlier) | The nonprofit version |
|---|---|
| Her name on the company | **Her seat on the board.** Nobody owns a nonprofit |
| A loan, written, with a date | **Still a loan** — a promissory note from the nonprofit to her, with a rate and a date, approved in the minutes. Or a donation, deductible, never repaid. She chooses; the loan keeps the brake |
| Secured on the building or option | Secured on the option or the lease, if the nonprofit holds one; a lender to a nonprofit can hold security |
| The brake — nothing moves until the seller and the state sign | **Unchanged.** Write it into the note |
| One number a quarter | **Unchanged** — and a nonprofit's Form 990 makes it public every year |

**The pair, still:** the nonprofit holds the mission, the measurement contract and the grants, and leases bays to a for-profit fleet company you own outright. The for-profit can take a 504 later. Form the nonprofit now — it is $59 and an evening — and let the determination letter run while the seller and the state answer.

## Every program and loan that could touch this — government and commercial

*Added 09-20 on his instruction: "include any possible government programs and commercial loans." Eligibility is the published rule as of today; every program is re-checked at application. Not advice.*

### Government money — grants and public loans

| Program | Who it pays | Fits which entity | The catch |
|---|---|---|---|
| **NHTSA §405d — impaired-driving countermeasure funds** (the National Priority Safety Program) | Flows to the **state** — Utah's Highway Safety Office files an Annual Grant Application (FY2026's is public) and subgrants it | The **nonprofit**, as a UHSO subgrantee running a countermeasure with a measurement plan | You do not apply to NHTSA; you get on UHSO's subgrant list. The memorandum in folder 04 is the door. Funds programs, rarely buildings |
| **NHTSA §402 — state highway-safety formula funds** | The state, same office | Same — subgrant | Same door; broader purposes |
| **USDOT SMART grants** — smart-community demonstration projects | **Public agencies only** (state, county, city, tribe, MPO); nonprofits and companies as *partners* | Salt Lake County or a city as the applicant, the nonprofit as the named partner | Requires a government sponsor; a county that wants the closing-time data might. Rounds are periodic |
| **USDA Rural Development — Community Facilities direct loan and grant** | Nonprofits and public bodies, for essential community facilities | The nonprofit — **but only in a rural place: population ≤ 20,000** | **Midvale fails the population test.** Wendover, Tooele County's small towns, the rural corridors pass. The rural proof site could be USDA-financed; the Salt Lake depot cannot |
| **USDA Community Facilities guaranteed loan** | Same purposes through a commercial lender with a USDA guarantee | Same rural test | Same |
| **SBA 504** (from the depot brief) | For-profit owner-occupants at 51% | **The for-profit fleet company only.** Nonprofits are ineligible | The pair structure exists for this reason |
| **SBA 7(a)** | For-profit small businesses; working capital, equipment, real estate | The for-profit only; **nonprofits are expressly ineligible** | Cars and chargers could be 7(a)-financed by the fleet company |
| **State of Utah** — Governor's Office of Economic Opportunity, rural programs; Utah Office of Outdoor Recreation and others | Varies; mostly for-profit job creation or rural infrastructure | Both, case by case | Check each cycle; none is a fit on its face for a public-safety fleet |
| **Private foundations** (highway-safety, public-health, alcohol-industry-funded responsibility foundations) | Nonprofits with a measurement plan | The nonprofit | The state's memorandum makes the application; without the number there is nothing to fund |

### Commercial and community lenders

| Lender type | Lends to | On what | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Utah CDFIs** — Rocky Mountain Community Reinvestment Corporation, Salt Lake Neighborhood Housing Services, Community Development Fund of Utah, Utah Microenterprise Loan Fund | Nonprofits and small businesses | Community facilities, acquisition and refinance; small business capital | The nonprofit's path to the depot. Terms by quote; LTV and board requirements are the questions in the brief |
| **Community banks and credit unions** (Goldenwest and City Center are CDFI-designated) | Both entities | Commercial real estate, equipment | Conventional terms: 20–30% down typical on commercial property; a nonprofit needs the determination letter and two years of financials, which it will not have — so a guarantor or the for-profit borrows |
| **Seller financing / lease-option** | Whoever the owner will sign with | The building itself | The cheapest money in the deal if the owner will carry it. First call |
| **Equipment finance** for the cars | The for-profit | The vehicles | Manufacturer or bank financing; the cars need not be bought with building money |
| **Program-related investments** from a foundation | The nonprofit | Below-market loans for mission work | Rare, real, and exactly shaped for a measured public-safety pilot |

### The shape that survives all of it
- **Nonprofit:** the mission, the UHSO memorandum and subgrant, foundation grants, a CDFI facility loan, a USDA loan *only for a rural site*. Cannot use SBA.
- **For-profit fleet company:** the cars (7(a) or equipment finance), the depot if owner-occupied at 51% (504), the lease from the nonprofit.
- **Andrea's $50k:** a loan to whichever entity holds the option, with the brake — or the nonprofit's first restricted gift, if that is what she wants it to be.
- **Order:** nonprofit filed tonight ($59) → seller call → UHSO email → CDC and CDFI calls → the 1023 → then the applications above, in the order their doors open.
