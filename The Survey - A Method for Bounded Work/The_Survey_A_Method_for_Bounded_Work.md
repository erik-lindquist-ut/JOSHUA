# The Survey — a method for bounded work

*A method for holding several bodies of work at once without letting them contaminate one another. It is a survey rather than a map: a map depicts terrain, a survey establishes boundaries and produces a description precise enough to convey to someone else.*

---

## 1 · Zones, plots, and the line between them

A **zone** is a domain of work with its own rules about what may enter and leave. A **plot** is a subdivision inside a zone. The distinction that makes the whole method work is this: **a parent names its children and their boundaries, and never their contents.**

That single rule governs indexes, logs, and conversation alike. It is what allows a body of work to be genuinely separable and genuinely contained at the same time.

**Containment without merger.** A zone sits inside a larger zone and keeps its own boundary, its own index, and its own record. This repeats at every scale — a workspace inside a workspace, a subdivision inside a domain, an option inside a decision. Every level is a fence around something that is still inside something else.

## 2 · The lock

Passage between zones is not a bridge. It is a lock: **entry, closure, deliberate level change, release.**

A bridge only opens or closes. A lock does the work in between — nothing is pushed across a boundary; it is brought to the far side's level first, and then let through.

| Rule | What it requires before acting |
|---|---|
| **The portability test** | Does this describe *how the work is done*, or *what a domain owns*? Only the former travels |
| **Direction** | One direction is ordinarily sanctioned; the other requires explicit authorisation. **Default deny** |
| **Finality** | The fork is one-way and permanent. It does not re-merge |
| **Slips** | A boundary slip caught at the gate is the system working. That is what the gate is for |
| **Proof** | The lock is only real if it holds when a crossing is requested directly |

**A crossing that is never recorded cannot be shown to have held.** Every release should log what crossed, in which direction, on whose authority, and — the column that matters — **what was withheld**. The withheld column is the evidence.

## 3 · Abutments

Every unit of work records where its edge meets another's. Four columns, and no fifth:

| Abuts | At what edge | What may cross | What never crosses |
|---|---|---|---|

**An abutment is a shared line, walked and marked. It is not a merger.** Naming one does not open it; several should be recorded as sealed, with the reason attached. A boundary that has never been walked is not a boundary — it is an assumption.

## 4 · The readout

Zones are not only containers. Read radially, they are a **diagnostic**: each sector reports on the health of what it holds, and the centre is the one element permitted in every sector — the person whose judgment is the only thing that actually crosses.

This yields something a set-overlap diagram cannot: **an address.** Depth and sector together locate a thing. A record of decisions needs an address, because a decision without a location in the structure cannot be found again.

**Two statuses that are not failures:**

- **Stale by design** — a unit opened with its record and its next step written, waiting for real work. A stale entry there is correct.
- **Absent by design** — a boundary deliberately holding nothing. Record the emptiness so it reads as a decision rather than an oversight. **An empty sector on a survey is still surveyed.**

## 5 · Terms mean what their sector says

The same term can resolve differently on either side of a boundary. When it does, the abbreviation is an unlabelled boundary and must be replaced by the words themselves.

This is why a flat glossary fails. One entry with a footnote is the wrong shape; two linked entries, each addressed to its own sector, is the right one. **A term is not defined — it is located.**

## 6 · The record

| Rule | Why |
|---|---|
| **Open the record at inception, never retrofit** | Retrofitting loses the wrong turns, and the wrong turns are the asset |
| **The artifact is the source of truth, not recollection** | Working from memory of a standard is unauditable and decays silently |
| **One source of truth per artifact** | One generator feeding many surfaces. Two hand-maintained copies drift, and the copy nobody reads is the one that rots |
| **Mark what was inferred** | An inferred value must never pass as a stated one |
| **Register decisions with their authority** | The difference between a decision and a habit is whether anyone authorised it |
| **Dated snapshots, never silent overwrites** | The set of snapshots is the difference |
| **Do not rewrite the record to match a new frame** | A record says what it said when it was written. Retro-editing history to fit a better idea is corruption wearing the clothes of tidiness |

## 7 · Good fences

The boundary that is maintained when nothing is currently pressing against it is the one that holds when something is. A wall with no present function, mended anyway, is not waste — the mending is the discipline, and the discipline is what remains when a crossing is finally requested.

Before building a boundary, be able to say what is being kept in and what is being kept out. A boundary whose purpose cannot be stated is inherited, not chosen — and an inherited boundary will not survive the first argument against it.

---

## Distillations

- The canal is its locks: controlled passage, one gate at a time.
- A lock equalises before it releases.
- A parent names its children and their boundaries, never their contents.
- An abutment is a shared line, not a merger.
- An empty sector on a survey is still surveyed.
- A term is not defined; it is located.
- The withheld column is the evidence.
- A boundary that has never been walked is an assumption.
