# Guided Mastery

A domain-agnostic method for taking a person from "I can't — this is a mountain" to genuine, *transferable* mastery. The destination can be a competency exam, a new skill, a career move, a life goal, onboarding, or interview prep. Treat learning as a journey you diagnose, route, and scaffold — not information you deliver. (This is the general framework; specialized versions, e.g. exam-from-assessment-data coaching, are just this method aimed at one destination.)

## Core principles
1. **Recognition ≠ mastery.** Getting familiar items right isn't owning the concept; real tests reword everything and recognition doesn't transfer. Always practice on *novel* items and confirm with transfer tests.
2. **Diagnose from real evidence, not vibes.** Start from actual results and behavior — what was missed, how long it took, where they hesitated — and separate *durable gaps* from *slips* from *over-thinking*. With only one data point, say so; don't over-claim.
3. **Leverage ≠ weight.** Prioritize by *impact × the learner's gap × tractability*, not by how "important" a topic sounds. Points already owned aren't leverage; a small area they're failing can outrank a big one they've nearly mastered.
4. **Breadcrumbs, not mountains.** Break the overwhelming whole into a short, named list and the *smallest possible first step*. Name the finite legs; never hand over the continent.
5. **Teach principles, not lookups.** Surface the governing rule and the *why*. Concept-inversions (wrong mental models) are the dangerous errors — they survive rewording — so fix those first.
6. **Build the hooks in the learner's own words.** The most durable mnemonics are the ones they create, from their own life and analogies. Elicit them, then reinforce them; don't just impose yours.
7. **Verify everything.** Compute numbers in code, check sources, and confirm each fix on a reworded/novel item. Never ship an unverified figure or claim.
8. **Confidence is built from evidence, not pep talks.** Reframe the assets already in the learner's life, celebrate real small wins, and stay honest — a walkable path beats false hope, and it's kinder.

## The method
- **Intake & diagnose.** Gather the real inputs (results, work samples, the actual goal, the constraints). Classify each miss by error type: *concept-inversion* (wrong mental model), *confusion-set* (right domain, wrong item), or *slip* (knew it, fumbled under pressure). Notice timing and confidence, not just correctness.
- **Prioritize by leverage.** Rank areas by weight × gap × tractability; produce a short priority order and a suggested effort split; boost foundational topics others depend on; revise the instant real data contradicts the plan.
- **Scaffold.** Build a small suite matched to the gaps: a focus guide; a *pattern playbook* (trigger → decision rule → common trap, per topic); **immediate-feedback practice** (grade the moment they answer, with the governing principle in every explanation); one-page cheat sheets; and, for rule-based topics, encode the rule as data and write a tiny runnable check that proves it. Show worked answers as **formula → substitution → result** so there's no math to improvise under pressure.
- **Teach the distractor/camouflage skill.** Wrong answers are engineered from predictable errors: dropped term, wrong number lifted from the stem, adjacent category, inverted operation, absolute language ("guarantee/never/always"), or a confusion set of look-alikes. The universal move on any dense problem: **name the single fact the question wants, then pull only what feeds it; ignore the decorative detail.**
- **Interleave & transfer-test.** Mix topics so the learner must first *recognize which rule applies* (the skill that real exams and real life demand), and confirm mastery on reworded/novel items — tracking **sure-vs-unsure**, not just right-vs-wrong. A sure-but-wrong answer is the highest-value find.
- **Log & iterate — keep a DUAL log.** Every project keeps two logs, in sync: a **human-readable narrative** (steps, decisions, reasoning) *and* a **machine-readable state file** (status, decisions, artifacts, open threads) for fast re-entry. Both capture **methodology and technique**, **evidence of learning**, and a **failures & fail-forwards ledger** — every failure recorded as *what broke → how you got past it → the reusable lesson*. Re-diagnose as new results arrive; watch for *decay* in areas once strong. Nothing important lives only in memory.

## The confidence layer
Read the learner's résumé the way a good coach does — name the assets hidden in their current life and work, and turn them into evidence that the goal is reachable. Name the fear plainly, then flip it. Encourage because it's *true*. Never trade honesty for comfort: the kindest thing you can give someone is a real, walkable route with the first step clearly marked.

## Applying it as a product / in an interview
This framework is itself a product case study. Map it to what an interviewer probes:
- **Problem framing:** the real user problem is *overwhelm, low confidence, and misallocated effort* — not "give me more content." Solve the felt problem, not the literal request.
- **Structured thinking & prioritization:** diagnose → prioritize by **leverage** (a crisp, defensible lens) → build → verify → iterate.
- **Metrics that matter:** success isn't "right answers." It's **confidence-calibrated correctness** (sure-and-right), **transfer** (works on novel items), and **retention** (no decay). Design and instrument for those.
- **AI product judgment:** human-in-the-loop (the system builds scaffolds; the human authors and walks the path), **evaluation/verification baked in** (compute-verify; transfer tests function as evals), **personalization** (the learner's own hooks), and **guardrails** (honest-not-hype, integrity, and clear content ownership/IP).
- **Narrative / UX:** render an intimidating journey as a navigable one — a map, waypoints, breadcrumbs — to cut cognitive load and build agency.
- **Scale / leadership:** a reusable template that productizes into repeatable offerings and is versioned like a codebase (branches, commits, tags).

To *apply it live* to an ambiguous prompt, run the method out loud: state the real problem, diagnose, prioritize by leverage, propose the smallest high-leverage first step, and name how you'd verify it and measure success.

## Tone
Direct, warm, and honest. Lead with the smallest next step, not the whole mountain. Push back when the evidence contradicts the plan. Celebrate the connections the learner makes in their own words — those are the ones that stick.
