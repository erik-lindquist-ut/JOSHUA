## B · Ride at Closing Utah — the nonprofit that proves it

**Size:** Utah spends **about $1.9 million a year of federal drunk-driving money** through its Highway Safety Office, in projects of **$50,000 to $495,000 each** (2026 plan). The biggest one — $495,000 — is a media campaign aimed at **men 21–39**, and the state's own survey says they don't know their **"sober ride choices."** That sentence is the door. A nonprofit is the only shape that can walk through it. **From the source, exact:** the impaired-driving section totals $1,402,868 of 405d money; $1,911,661 counting the office's two admin lines; every subgrantee in that section is a state agency — nonprofits appear elsewhere in the plan under 402/405b (Utah Safety Council, Bike Utah, Safe Kids Utah). Source: [Utah FY2026 grant application](https://www.nhtsa.gov/sites/nhtsa.gov/files/2026-01/UT_FY26_AGA.pdf), pages 33–39.

**File the nonprofit corporation using the state's template**  
**From the source** — [Utah Division of Corporations — nonprofit](https://commerce.utah.gov/corporations/business-entities/domestic-non-profit-corporation/): $59, not refundable. Online: sign in with a UtahID → Formations → Domestic Nonprofit Corporation. The form asks for name, purpose, a registered agent with a Utah street address, the incorporator, whether there are voting members, the main address, and the directors — the state's guide says **at least 3**. From Jan 1, 2025 you upload your latest IRS 990 at each renewal (a new group uploads at its first renewal). Renewal is $18 a year, $10 more if late; $75 extra to expedite ([fee list](https://corporations.utah.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/currentfees.pdf)). Everything filed is public record.

**Ask three people to be the board**  
**From the source** — [Utah Nonprofits Association — FAQ](https://www.utahnonprofits.org/nonprofit-faqs/): the FAQ doesn't set a board minimum; the state's own guide does — three directors. UNA has a 2024 "Guide to Starting a Nonprofit" PDF on its [Starting a Nonprofit](https://www.utahnonprofits.org/starting-a-nonprofit/) page; UNA phone (801) 596-1800. It does not do fiscal sponsorship.

**Apply to the IRS to be tax-exempt**  
**From the source** — [IRS — 1023-EZ / 1023](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-1023-ez): short form $275 on Pay.gov; you must expect $50,000 or less coming in each of the next three years, the same for the past three, and $250,000 or less in assets ([worksheet in the instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1023ez)). An LLC can't use the short form. IRS decides 80% of short forms within **22 days**, long forms within **191 days** ([Where's my application](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/charitable-organizations/wheres-my-application-for-tax-exempt-status), June 2026). Long form $600 ([about 1023](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-1023)). While waiting you still file a 990 and tick "application pending." Status line 877-829-5500; approval shows up first in the IRS's Tax Exempt Organization Search.

**Get the federal UEI number**  
**From the source** — [SAM.gov — entity registration](https://sam.gov/content/entity-registration): free. For a UEI only (what a sub-grantee needs) you give the legal name and street address. To apply for federal money directly you need the full registration — up to 10 business days to go active, renewed every 365 days. Make a Login.gov account first. Free help: fsd.gov live chat and phone, Mon–Fri 8–8 Eastern.

**Each year, upload the IRS return when you renew with the state**  
**From the source** — [Utah Consumer Protection — charities](https://dcp.utah.gov/for-businesses/charities/): H.B. 43 (2024) ended the yearly charity registration on May 1, 2024; from Jan 1, 2025 the 990 is uploaded to the Division of Corporations instead. Consumer Protection still enforces the law and can fine a group that skips the upload — questions to dcpregistrations@utah.gov. The small-group 990 is the [990-N e-Postcard](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/annual-electronic-filing-requirement-for-small-exempt-organizations-form-990-n-e-postcard): free, online only, for groups normally taking in $50,000 or less (first year: $75,000 or less), due the 15th day of the fifth month after year-end (May 15 for a calendar year); miss it three years running and tax-exempt status ends automatically.

**After the IRS letter, get the Utah sales-tax exemption**  
**From the source** — [Utah Tax Commission — TC-160](https://files.tax.utah.gov/tax/forms/current/tc-160.pdf): apply online at tap.utah.gov ("Apply for a Religious and Charitable Sales Tax Exemption Number"); attach the IRS letter; give the EIN and a short statement of purpose and how you raise money, in your own words. Doesn't cover unrelated business sales. Questions 801-297-2200. An officer signs under penalty of perjury.

## E · The first car — Andrea buys it for JOSHUA Holdings

**Size:** one car at one bar door costs about **$41 a night** to run; three cars covering a county for a year is about **$45,000** — which is why $50,000 is the exact size of a county pilot, not a coincidence. Her money is the first car; it is never the building.

**Pick the car for the job**  
**From the source** — [Utah Code 41-26 — self-driving cars](https://le.utah.gov/xcode/Title41/Chapter26/41-26.html): a Level 4 or 5 car may drive with no human aboard if the system can follow Utah traffic law, meets federal safety standards, and can pull itself to a safe stop if it fails. The driving system is legally the "operator" and needs no licence (amended May 2026). The car is titled, registered and insured like any car — no special state permit. **A robotaxi fleet operates under the existing for-hire rules** (step 49). Level 3 needs a licensed human ready to take over.

**Buy and title the car**  
**From the source** — [Utah DMV — commercial vehicles](https://dmv.utah.gov/register/vehicles/commercial/): a sedan is "commercial" by use, not weight. Title with form TC-656 (every owner shows government ID); first Utah title needs a VIN inspection (TC-661) unless bought new from a Utah dealer; safety and emissions certificates if required; renew yearly. Fees aren't on the page — use the [DMV fee estimator](https://mvp.tax.utah.gov) or call 801-297-7780; Salt Lake County adds a $10 corridor fee and a $3 air fee.

**Buy commercial for-hire insurance in JOSHUA's name**  
**From the source** — [insurance minimums — state and city](https://le.utah.gov/xcode/Title31A/Chapter22/31A-22-S304.html): Utah's floor for any car is $30,000 per person / $65,000 per crash / $25,000 property. The state sets no separate for-hire number — **Salt Lake City does**: its ground-transportation code (5.71.140) requires the federal motor-carrier level, **$1.5 million per vehicle** for 15 seats or fewer, with the city named as additional insured ([SLC code](https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/saltlakecityut/latest/saltlakecity_ut/0-0-0-48486)). UDOT's private-carrier rule is $750,000. **Plan on a $1.5 million commercial policy; get the quote before the car.**

**Ask Midvale and Salt Lake City licensing what a for-hire car service needs**  
**From the source** — [Utah Consumer Protection — TNC rule · Salt Lake City ground transportation](https://dcp.utah.gov/for-businesses/transportation-network-companies/): a TNC is an app company that matches riders to drivers **and does not own, control, operate or manage the cars** — so a company that owns its cars is not a TNC and doesn't pay the $5,000 TNC registration. GT office 801-908-7204, SLCAirportGT@slc.gov. A car based in Midvale that picks up in Salt Lake City needs this; Midvale's own answer is still step 49's phone call.

## H · The sky — "state satellites," said plainly

**Size:** ten western states are the pin. Each one has the same federal drunk-driving pot with its own office (step 52, ten times over). The sky layer is what lets one county's proof travel to ten states — but **you don't build the satellites; you rent the sky that's already there, and buy the pictures from the people who got there first.** Only one line here (step 65) is an actual satellite, and it's a data business with a buyer, not a fleet requirement.

**Buy the road first: one Starlink Mini (the car), one Starlink Business dish (the depot), and T-Mobile's satellite-to-phone add-on for riders**  
**From the source** — [Starlink Business · T-Mobile T-Satellite](https://www.starlink.com/business): Business: $349 Standard kit; Local Priority 50 GB $55/month, 500 GB $155, 1 TB $280, 2 TB $530; +50 GB for $25; after priority data runs out, unlimited at 1 Mbps down; priority plans include a public IP, 24/7 support, in-motion use (as of Sept 2026). Car: Roam plans in step 50. Rider's phone — [T-Satellite](https://www.t-mobile.com/coverage/satellite-phone-service): $10/month per line (free on some T-Mobile plans; non-customers can buy it with an unlocked phone), texting, picture messages, location sharing, text-to-911, and data **only through an approved app list** (Google Maps, WhatsApp, X and others — a ride app is not on it today); needs a clear view of the sky.

**Register the depot's own small cell in the shared band**  
**From the source** — [FCC — 3.5 GHz CBRS · 47 CFR 96.39](https://www.fcc.gov/wireless/bureau-divisions/mobility-division/35-ghz-band/35-ghz-band-overview): three tiers: federal users, county licences sold at auction (PAL), and General Authorized Access (GAA). **GAA is licensed-by-rule: no FCC application, no FCC fee.** Every radio must register with a Spectrum Access System before it transmits — Google and Federated Wireless are approved through Jan 2030 (also Sony, Key Bridge, RED); the SAS charges its own monthly per-radio fee. A Certified Professional Installer must install and register high-power radios and any radio mounted above 6 m. You may not interfere with the tiers above, and other GAA users may not protect you.

## J · The other three doors — detox, elderly care, medicine — the Tesla-enabled core of JOSHUA

**Size — detox:** Utah lost **617 people to overdoses in 2024 and 601 in 2025** (state health department). Utah made **10,412 DUI arrests in 2022**, and the courts send those people to treatment — the same men the closing-time car picks up. **Utah Medicaid pays for adult social detox** under a federal waiver, so a licensed operator has a payer on day one. Salt Lake County's main detox is one nonprofit (Volunteers of America); you call for a bed.
