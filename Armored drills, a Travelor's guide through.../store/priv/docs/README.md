## One sentence

A working method—and the book that proves it—that turns competency-based assessment data into mastery that survives rewording, not recognition that dies on the objective exam.

## Why this matters to WGU

WGU’s model lives or dies on whether a learner *owns* a principle or merely *recognized* a familiar item. Fail Fast is the learner’s answer to that gap: take the pre-assessment early, let it fail you where you are weak, rank study by **leverage** (weight × gap × speed), then prove every fix with a transfer item that the practice bank never asked.

This Compendium is the first full course-scale instantiation of that method in **Accounting for Decision Making**, built from one learner’s real PA and OA coaching reports—not from a syllabus outline.

## The method (Provost-level)

1. **Fail early.** Pre-assess before you feel ready. The miss list *is* the study plan.
2. **Classify the miss.** Concept-inversion (dangerous) vs. confusion-set vs. compute slip.
3. **Prioritize by leverage,** not topic weight alone. Foundational topics that unlock others get a boost.
4. **Armor the drill.** Re-ask every concept with more camouflage than the practice item; label every distractor’s engineered error; transfer items for every miss.
5. **Prove transfer.** If they can only answer the original phrasing, it isn’t mastery.
