2. THE SCHOOL VERSION

- Schools are designed, not remodeled. This goes in the drawings before the concrete is poured.
- No carpets. The whole floor is hardened around each student's work space.
- The student's desk is the Tesla tile. The dog stands into it.
- The teacher pulls zones down for whole-class time, raises single stations when a student needs to work apart, and table groups are the default.
- Halls are doing a protection job. (Pinned, not designed.)
- The way out is a marked "yellow brick road," with a facilitator attached to it. (Pinned, not designed.)
- Name for the single-station mode: "air-gapped island." Also fine: "study carrel." Never use "seclusion" - in schools that word is legally loaded and pulls in restraint policy and special-ed review.
- No chairs also means no chair storage, no stacking, no floor scraping, and no clearing the room for an assembly.
- Maintenance: custodians learn it on the job, the way HVAC controls and badge readers came into buildings. Two things make that work: leg and zone parts that unbolt and swap as modules (no carbon repair on site), and training bid as a line item during commissioning.

------------------------------------------------------------
3. THE CORE IDEA

A work surface with no chairs. A center spine (the dog) positions every work zone. Each zone sticks out from the exoskeleton it stands in, so no surface has legs under it, and a wheelchair can pull straight in from either side.

Each zone sets its own height:
- Young children: 20-22 in
- Wheelchair: surface at or below 34 in, with 27 in of open knee space below
- Standing bar: 40-42 in

Postures the dog takes: standing bar, kid height, wheelchair, picnic, cot.

------------------------------------------------------------
4. THE FOLD AND THE FLOOR

- The top folds inward. The underside folds inward as a shell. The whole thing tucks flush into a hardened box set between floor joists. When it's down, the room is open floor.
- He has already built three boxes like this in a home remodel: framed, flush, powered for charging, seams hidden except where they double as cold-air return and heating vents. A vent is supposed to have lines in the floor, so the seam looks normal instead of hidden.
- The three boxes together seat about 28 people. The direction of the house doesn't matter; the layout of boxes defines the room.

------------------------------------------------------------
5. THE RIBS (part of the exoskeleton)

- Carbon fiber. Same members do three jobs: shell when stowed, frame when standing, and the surface the solar skin stretches over.
- Load path: weight goes into the ribs, through a locked column, into the floor. The dog holds still instead of holding up.
- Carbon fiber cautions: it doesn't bend before it breaks, so build past the math and test past the use. Bolt holes through the layers start cracks - every fold joint needs a metal fitting bonded into the layup. It needs UV protection outdoors; the solar skin covers part of that.

------------------------------------------------------------
6. LEGS AND LOCKS

- Elbows down, push-up position, when standing.
- Gravity drops telescoping legs for free, and each one finds its own ground, so it levels itself on uneven floors.
- Servos set the height. Something mechanical (pin, collar, or detent) holds it. The servo rests, and a dead battery leaves the surface where it was.

------------------------------------------------------------
7. POWER AND SUMMONS

- Solar panels on the shell underside, which faces up when stowed.
- Rough numbers: 15-20 W per sq ft in sun. A 3x6 ft shell makes ~300 W at noon and 1.5-2 kWh on a good day - enough to refill a Spot battery with room to spare.
- Triggers: programmed logic, Bluetooth switch, timer, or a house power switch.
- A local stop at the box. When something rises out of a floor with people on it, someone in the room needs a stop that isn't a phone.

------------------------------------------------------------
8. SPOT - REFERENCE FACTS

- Payload about 31 lb. About 90 minutes on a ~600 Wh battery. It balances; it doesn't brace.
- No public price (quote-only). Configured units in public buying records run $150k-$375k.
- Boston Dynamics sells mainly to businesses, government, and research groups.

10. BACKGROUND - THE RIBBON PRODUCT VENTURE (separate track)

This is the earlier product line, before the dog-as-spine pin. Keep it apart from the school version unless he joins them.
- RIBBON: a chair-free communal table system for 14 people, built from self-supporting ribbon segments at different heights.
- Naming: ribbon = the surface you can compose, thread = the invisible protocol, table = the human outcome. Story arc for investors: thread -> ribbon -> table -> family.
- Terms: Segments, Runs, the Knot (fixed focal segment), seam pieces, bays (740 mm, wheelchair-first), AEL family (optional perches, not chairs), delta-class caps. Ring and Hex layouts. Base requirements B1-B13.
- Height range: 560-1100 mm. Colorways: Cloud, Grove, Citrus, Slate, Custom, on a navy/teal base.
- Current revision: Rev H.1 (pre-phase-0). It retired an older one-piece "monolith" design for a modular splice design.
- Starting market: US West Coast / SoCal.
- Artifacts exist: business plan, 14-slide pitch deck, one-pager, financial model, PRD-01 to PRD-14, build spec, plan-view drawings, latch decision brief, freight RFQ, supplier shortlist, pilot packet.
