# V4.1 PUNCH LIST — one step at a time, one choice at a time, and what each choice does to the next step

*V3.0 hid choices inside steps. This one doesn't. Every step is **one action**. If the action contains a choice, the choice is named, the options are listed, the **default** is marked (the smallest correct one — take it unless you have a reason), and **what it does to the next step** is spelled out. Every step ends with **Lost? →** the one page to open. Nothing on this page is legal or tax advice; the state, the IRS and the lender decide — this is the order to ask them in.*

*V4.1 (2026-09-23): steps 1–5 are done — the company is **LAST TEN YARDS LLC** (JOSHUA HOLDINGS was taken, active since 2003); no DBA; EIN letter saved. Bank = Relay. Andrea is out of the LLC, the nonprofit and the car — she can't sign anything. Venture email: erik.lindquist.ut@gmail.com.*

*Steps run strictly in order. Step N needs only step N−1. If you stop at any step, nothing below it has cost you anything.*

## Before step 1 — the one decision that shapes every step after it

**Who owns the company on the day it's filed?**

| Option | What it means | What it does to steps 1–7 |
|---|---|---|
| **A · You alone, today (default)** | A single-member LLC. You're the only owner and the only signer. Founders join later by a written amendment when they say yes. | Every filing below is yours to do this week. The operating agreement (step 6) includes one clause: *new members may be admitted by written amendment.* No waiting on anyone. |
| **B · The founders from day one** | A multi-member LLC — B.A., K.A., A.A. and you (or not you) as owners at filing. | Nothing below can be filed until all three have said yes in writing, agreed on percentages, and signed the operating agreement. The tax ID (step 5) names one "responsible party." The bank (step 7) needs every owner's ID. Weeks, not days. |

**Default: A.** Reason a second-grader can check: the founders haven't said yes, and a company that exists can be handed to them; a company that doesn't exist can't be handed to anyone. Converting A to B later is a signed amendment and a bank form, not a new company.

*Lost? →* [Utah — what an LLC is and how to form one](https://commerce.utah.gov/corporations/business-entities/domestic-limited-liability-company/)

**10. File the nonprofit corporation online, using the state's template. $59.**

**14. Apply to the IRS to be tax-exempt.**
The choice: **short form (1023-EZ, $275) or long form (1023, $600).** Take the IRS's eligibility worksheet; it decides. The two questions that matter for you: *will it take in more than $50,000 a year in any of the next three years? will it own more than $250,000 in assets?* **Default: short form**, because the nonprofit owns no car and no building — those are the company's.
What it does to step 15 and to the grant later: short form = a letter in about 22 days for most filers; long form = about 191 days. No letter, no grant.
Done when: the acknowledgment; then the determination letter.
*Lost? →* [IRS — Form 1023-EZ and the eligibility worksheet](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-1023-ez) · [IRS — Form 1023](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-1023)
