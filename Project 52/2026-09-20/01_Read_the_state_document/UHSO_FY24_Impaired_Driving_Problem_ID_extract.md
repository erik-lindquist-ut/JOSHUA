# Utah Highway Safety Office — Impaired Driving Program, Problem Identification FY24 (extract)

*Public document, fetched 2026-09-19 from highwaysafety.utah.gov. Facts as stated there; nothing added.*

- Utah's .05 BAC law in effect since 2018-12-30 — the country's first.
- 2017–2021: alcohol-related fatal crashes were 15% of motor-vehicle fatalities while only 1.5% of crashes; 11.9× more likely to be deadly. ~41 deaths a year on average. 2021: 55 alcohol-related fatal crashes, 61 deaths.
- Drug-positive drivers: ~100 deaths a year; 36% of traffic fatalities 2017–2021.
- **When:** alcohol crashes peak Friday–Sunday, 6:00 p.m.–1:00 a.m., June–December.
- **Where:** most common along the Wasatch Front (Ogden to Provo). Highest alcohol-crash rates: Salt Lake, Utah, Davis, Weber. Highest fatal rates: Salt Lake and Utah Counties.
- **DUI arrests:** 10,412 in 2022 (10,619 in 2021). 66% on the Wasatch Front (Salt Lake, Davis, Utah, Weber); 34% outside. 71% first-time offenders. Average BAC .14. 74% male; 21–39 the core ages.
- **Drowsy-driving fatal crashes** highest in Millard, Salt Lake, **Tooele**, and Utah Counties — the long empty roads.
- MADD figure cited: the average drunk driver has driven impaired over 80 times before an arrest.
